# Slide 4 — Feasibility and Viability

Fixed title: "FEASIBILITY AND VIABILITY."

This slide is where a reviewer checks whether the team has actually thought past the exciting part of the idea into whether it can really be built, afforded, operated, and adopted. It rewards honesty about real challenges paired with real mitigation more than it rewards claiming everything is easy.

## The near-universal 4-quadrant core

Almost every deck examined splits feasibility into four buckets, each its own colored card/column:

- **Technical Feasibility** — is the core technology mature and buildable with realistic tools/timelines? (e.g. "technically mature and scalable," "achievable with existing AI/ML frameworks.")
- **Financial / Economic Feasibility** — what does this cost to build and run, and where does funding come from? (government schemes, grants, CSR/PPP partnerships, phased rollout to control upfront cost.)
- **Operational Feasibility** — can it actually run day to day — integrate into existing workflows, get adopted by non-technical users, get maintained? (training programs, simple dashboards, minimal staff training.)
- **Social / Market Viability** — will the people it's meant for actually want and trust it? (builds trust, preserves/promotes relevant culture or community practice, long-term acceptance.)

Each bucket gets 2-4 short bullet points or a short paragraph, bold-lead-phrase formula, color-coded consistently with that bucket's color everywhere else on the slide. Team-specific variants exist — sometimes "Viability" is broken out separately with its own Market/Financial/Sustainability sub-split, or the four buckets appear as horizontal bars instead of vertical columns — but the same four underlying questions (can we build it / can we afford it / can we run it / will it be adopted) are always present in some form.

## Challenges paired with strategies

A second block, usually positioned alongside or beneath the four-quadrant core, explicitly pairs each challenge with its mitigation. Three layouts recur:

1. **Diamond-bulleted two-column list** — challenges in one column, strategies in the matching row of the next column.
2. **Grouped colored panels by category** — e.g. a "Technical Challenges" panel and a "Financial Challenges" panel, each listing 2-4 bold-lead-phrase strategy bullets underneath its own challenges.
3. **A formal table** — Challenge / Risk column, Strategy column, sometimes with a numbered risk index.

Naming a real challenge honestly ("Lack of a diverse, labeled dataset makes robust model training difficult") followed immediately by a credible strategy ("Create a proprietary dataset via data-sharing partnerships with local mills") reads as far more mature than skipping challenges altogether.

## Market sizing and financial detail (product/market-facing ideas)

When the idea targets a real commercial or civic market, include:

- **TAM/SAM/SOM table or diagram** — Total Addressable Market, Serviceable Addressable Market, Serviceable Obtainable Market, each with an actual calculated number (dollar figure or population figure) and, ideally, the one-line basis for that calculation shown alongside it (e.g. "affected population × % with technology access").
- A supporting **bar chart** for a multi-year revenue/growth projection, or a **pie/donut chart** for market-share breakdown, with values labeled directly on the chart.

## Comparison table (hardware/community/infrastructure ideas)

For ideas replacing or improving an existing approach, a compact comparison table — Existing approach vs. Proposed/Innovation — across concrete specs (scale, footprint, cost, portability, output quality) is a strong, easy-to-build substitute for market sizing when the idea isn't really a "market" play in the startup sense (e.g. community infrastructure, public-sector tools).

## Ask the user, if you don't already know

- What are the real, honest technical/financial/operational/adoption risks — not the polished version, the actual ones?
- For each risk, what's the team's actual planned mitigation?
- Is there a target market with real (even rough) size estimates, or is this better framed as a cost/benefit comparison against an existing approach?
- What funding sources are realistic for this specific idea (government scheme, CSR, grant, phased self-funded rollout)?
