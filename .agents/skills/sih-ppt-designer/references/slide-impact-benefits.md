# Slide 5 — Impact and Benefits

Fixed title: "IMPACT AND BENEFITS."

This is the closing argument of the deck — it needs to leave the reviewer convinced the idea matters at scale, to specific groups of real people, not just that it's technically clever. Specificity about *who* benefits and *how much* is what separates a strong version of this slide from a generic one.

## Two ways the content gets organized — often combined

### Stakeholder-segmented breakdown

Split the impact by *who* benefits. Common stakeholder buckets, pick the 3-5 that actually fit the idea: Users/Citizens/Tourists, Local Community, Government/Policymakers, the Environment, sometimes a Technology/Operations bucket for infrastructure-heavy ideas. Two layouts recur:

- A **hub-and-spoke diagram**: a central node like "Benefits of the Solution," with one labeled box per stakeholder group arranged around it, each containing 1-2 bold-lead-phrase bullets.
- Plain **quadrant cards**, one per stakeholder, each color-coded and containing its own short bullet list — functionally the same content, less diagrammatic.

### Benefits vs. Impacts split

An alternative (or complementary) two-column organization: **Benefits** = what's directly gained (cost savings, time saved, quality improvement), **Impacts** = the broader/measurable effect that follows from those benefits (fewer errors industry-wide, healthier communities, better policy data). Each bullet in both columns gets its own small icon reinforcing its meaning, plus the bold-lead-phrase treatment.

For larger or more mature ideas, both organizations can appear together on a denser slide: stakeholder-segmented Impact and Benefit categories (e.g. "Citizen Benefits," "Government Benefits," "Technological Benefits," "Social Benefits" as four more boxes) alongside a separate, more technical Impacts breakdown (e.g. "Speed & Reliability," "Smart Administration," "Smart Insights," "Transparency & Accessibility").

## Business / sustainability model block

Common on the right side of the slide, especially for civic, social, or public-facing ideas — a set of small labeled boxes covering:

- **Funding sources** — shown as a row of icons/short labels (government budgets, CSR/PPP partnerships, grants, tourism/usage-based levies).
- **Target customers/users** — who specifically pays for or uses this.
- **Value-added services** — the specific extra offerings for different user types (e.g. one line for what tourists get, one for what the community gets, one for what government gets).
- **Financial model** — how the specific funding sources translate into sustainability (e.g. "Government Schemes: central/state development funds," "CSR & PPP Models: tech companies and telecoms as partners reduce financial burden").
- **Scalability** — the specific rollout sequence from pilot to scale (e.g. "start with one/two hotspot districts, then expand statewide").

## Quantify hard here

This slide leans on numbers more than almost any other — percentage cost reductions, dollar figures, population reach, timeframes to ROI. SDG (Sustainable Development Goal) icons are worth including when the idea is genuinely sustainability-related — they're a recognizable, credible shorthand reviewers respond to.

## Closing credibility elements

Near the bottom of this slide is a common place for: a working-prototype or demo-video link, and — when the team genuinely has one — an industry mentor/referral contact block (name, company, years of experience, contact info). These function as a final trust signal right before the deck ends, so don't bury them; give them their own small clearly-labeled box.

## Ask the user, if you don't already know

- Who are the 3-5 specific stakeholder groups this idea actually changes something for, and what changes for each?
- What are the real (or realistically estimated) numbers behind the impact — cost savings, time saved, people reached, environmental effect?
- What's the actual funding/sustainability plan, and what's the realistic path from pilot to scale?
- Is there a mentor, industry contact, or working demo worth featuring as a closing credibility signal?
